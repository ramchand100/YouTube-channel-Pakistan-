## Important note before scripting

**The research surfaced a significant development the original angle didn't
anticipate, and it should be reflected in the script's framing.** The angle
was written on the premise of a policy that had been unveiled and now carried
a built-in tariff-phase-down risk. The reality found in research is more
dramatic and more current: **there is no new auto policy in force at all
right now.**

- The old policy (AIDEP 2021-26) expired June 30, 2026.
- A draft 2026-31 policy was prepared, then **shelved around August 3, 2026**
  after manufacturers objected it favored pure EVs so heavily it risked
  turning Pakistan into "an assembly point for imported EV kits" (C-09).
- The PM tasked a committee led by Ishaq Dar with a revised draft, which got
  **in-principle approval August 13** — but as of this research, it still
  hasn't reached the federal cabinet, let alone been gazetted (C-10, C-11).
- Meanwhile, the *policy vacuum itself* already did real damage: when the old
  policy expired, hybrid vehicles automatically lost their preferential GST
  rate and reverted to the standard 25% — Toyota and Honda raised hybrid
  prices by Rs 1.3-1.4 million overnight in July (C-14, C-15).

This is a stronger story than the original framing, not a weaker one — but
the central question should probably shift from "will the policy's timeline
undercut its own goal" to something like **"the policy fight that's already
costing consumers money before it's even been written"** — the paralysis
itself is the anomaly, and the eventual policy's tariff-timeline question
becomes Part 2's mechanism, not the opening hook. Recommend revisiting the
central question/thesis wording with `pakistan-topic-angles` briefly before
`pakistan-script-writer` drafts, rather than scripting against the original
framing as if the policy vacuum weren't part of the story.

## 1. Central paradox / hook

A brand-new hybrid car got **Rs 1.3-1.4 million more expensive overnight in
July 2026** — not because of a new tax anyone voted on, but because the
government's auto policy expired and nothing has replaced it yet, six weeks
after Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif was told the replacement was "nearing
approval" (C-01, C-14, C-15).

## 2. Ground-truth mechanics

- Pakistan's auto policies run on 5-year cycles (2016-21, then AIDEP
  2021-26). Each cycle sets import tariffs and, this time for the first time
  explicitly, localization percentage targets.
- The 2016-21 policy never actually set a percentage target for local
  content — it used a duty *differential* instead (10% customs duty on
  non-localized parts vs. 25% on localized parts, for five years) to make
  localizing more profitable than importing (C-23, directly verified against
  the primary BOI document).
- The 2026-31 draft is a real departure from that approach: explicit
  percentage targets (85% for two-wheelers/light vehicles by 2030, 80-90%
  for small cars by 2031) paired with a falling tariff ceiling (weighted
  average toward 7.4%, aspirational 6% net average, CBU capped at 15%) (C-02,
  C-03, C-04).
- Two ministries disagree on how fast the tariff ceiling should fall:
  Industries wants to pair any cut with a compensating excise duty to
  preserve *effective* protection; Commerce wants the National Tariff
  Policy's 15%-by-2030 cap applied without a carve-out (C-12). The IMF has
  already rejected one proposed workaround — a 1% NEV sales tax — and is
  holding firm on the standard 18% GST (C-13).

## 3. Core conflict / bottleneck

PAAPAM's central claim is concrete and quotable: Pakistani parts
manufacturers carry a **34% cost disadvantage** against regional rivals,
because none of the raw materials (auto-grade steel, plastic resin, rubber)
are produced domestically, on top of high energy costs, taxes, and borrowing
costs (C-07). Their position: cutting CBU tariffs to 15% *before* that
disadvantage is addressed "will cause the industry to collapse" — they want
50-60% CBU duty and a 45% floor on protection for localized parts instead
(C-06).

- **Who benefits from the standoff continuing:** existing assemblers and
  parts-makers, who keep effective protection levels while the fight drags
  on.
- **Who pays for it:** consumers, immediately and measurably — the hybrid
  price jump (C-14, C-15) is a direct, dated cost of the policy vacuum, not
  a hypothetical.

## 4. Broader macro impact

- PAAPAM frames the stakes as over 1 million livelihoods (300,000+ direct,
  700,000 indirect) — this is their own advocacy estimate, not an
  independent labor count, and must be attributed as such in the script
  (C-08).
- The historical pattern (2016-21 and 2021-26) shows Pakistan has run this
  exact "protect, then localize" experiment twice before with mixed results:
  the 2016-21 policy attracted 12+ announced new-manufacturer investments,
  but most were pulled or shelved amid currency depreciation and policy
  volatility (C-17); AIDEP 2021-26 hit its export targets for motorcycles
  and tractors (~150% of target) but missed them badly for cars, which
  stayed under 1% of production as exports (C-18). Both figures are
  REPORTED from trade/industry analysis, not confirmed against primary
  EDB/PAMA export data — flagged for a closer look before the script treats
  them as settled.
- Consumer protection under AIDEP 2021-26 did produce one concrete,
  verifiable win: Rs 5.3 billion recovered from manufacturers over delayed
  deliveries (C-19), though this is tangential to the localization question.

## 5. Counterarguments

**The strongest alternative explanation:** lower tariffs and faster exposure
to import competition could be the right medicine, not premature harm — the
"protection first" framing assumes protecting today's assemblers is the
correct goal, when a counter-view holds that only real competitive pressure
(not another protected window) will force genuine localization instead of
another repeat of 2016-21 and 2021-26's mixed results. PAAPAM's 34%
cost-disadvantage figure is real ammunition for the "give us more time"
side, but it's their own number, not an independently audited one — the
script should attribute it clearly rather than adopt it as settled fact, and
should let the counterargument stand on its own merits rather than treating
industry pushback as automatically correct.

## 6. Unresolved questions

- Whether the revised (Dar-committee) draft has since cleared the federal
  cabinet and been formally notified — this needs a fresh check immediately
  before scripting, since the situation was moving fast as of mid-August and
  the pipeline of committee → ministry → cabinet still had steps left as of
  the last available report (C-11).
- The exact current local-content percentage achieved by major assemblers
  today — no primary source with this figure was found; the AIDEP 2021-26
  primary PDF is a scanned/image document this pipeline can't OCR, so this
  gap should be closed by a human pass or an OCR-capable tool before the
  script states any specific current local-content percentage.
- Whether the sub-Rs 1 million PAVE electric car actually launched — its
  summer 2026 target window appears to have passed with no confirmation
  found (C-22).
- A clean, single-source figure for how much more Pakistani buyers pay vs.
  regional peers — the comparative claim in this brief (C-20) is
  qualitative/ANALYSIS-level, not a precise percentage.
